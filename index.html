Day 2 · Making the invisible visible

Every rack in this city is bending right now.

Pallet racking carries thousands of kilograms on steel beams a few millimetres thick. The stress inside that steel builds up pallet by pallet, week by week. A person walking the aisle sees nothing until the day it gives way.

This is one aisle. Six bays, three levels, a forklift on its morning run. Watch what nobody can see.

How it works

StressLens

1

Engineering first. A finite-element model of the rack says where stress concentrates: mid-span of each beam and the base of each upright. Sensors go there, and only there. One strain gauge and one accelerometer per critical point, not hundreds.

2

A living twin. Each reading drives a 3D copy of the aisle. Beams are coloured by utilisation, the ratio of today's bending moment to the beam's rated moment. Vibration traces catch loose connections and forklift impacts.

3

The rack speaks before it fails. At 90% the forklift screen refuses the put-away, the control room gets the alert, and a maintenance ticket opens with the exact bay and level.

Model in this demo: beams as simply supported spans of 3.9 m with three pallet positions, rated 2,500 kg per level spread evenly; pallets 1,000 kg (1,500 kg for the heavy one). Two pallets side by side concentrate their load near mid-span, so 2,000 kg reads 80%. Utilisation = max bending moment ÷ rated moment. All numbers are simulated.
